\label{sec:Introduction}

Particle detectors convert the traces of passing elementary particles interacting with the detector material into measurable signals. Different types of detectors allow the tracking, energy measurement, and identification of particles. As particle velocities are usually close to the speed of light and the interaction times are typically short, a high time resolution $\mathcal{O}(\si{\nano\second})$ is required for the readout of such detectors. When multiple readout channels or even multiple detectors are used, special emphasis must be put on the synchronization of the readout system, in order to correctly reconstruct events \cite{Book-KolanoskiWermes}.
\par The topic of the presented master's thesis is the development of a synchronous readout system and the event reconstruction of two different muon detectors: A \ac{DT} chamber, which is a large-volume gaseous detector, and a scintillation detector. The measurement uses atmospheric muons, created from interactions of cosmic rays in Earth's atmosphere and therefore continuously arriving at the surface \cite{PDG2024,CosmicMuon-Shukla}.
\par The Physics Institute III A at RWTH Aachen University operates a cosmic test stand with a \ac{DT} chamber. This chamber is of the same type as used in the \ac{CMS} experiment \cite{CMS-Main}, one of the two large multipurpose particle detectors at the \ac{LHC} accelerator \cite{LHC-Main} located at \acs{CERN}. The newly available Phase-2 readout electronics \cite{CMS-Phase2,CMS-Phase2Muon} for the \ac{CMS} \ac{DT} system were installed at the chamber at the test stand. In this work, a track reconstruction of atmospheric muons with the fully populated chamber is attempted for the first time with the new electronics.
% The test stand in Aachen is a remnant of the chamber construction for the experiment in the early 2000s, to which the institute significantly contributed \cite{RWTH-History}. After initially inactive when the chamber construction was completed, the test stand has been build up with all required infrastructure in the recent years.
\par Independently, a scintillation strip detector has been developed over several theses at the institute \cite{BSc-Ehlert,MSc-Ehlert}. This detector is composed of scintillation strips, which are read out by \acp{SiPM}. The construction of the detector was completed, and an initial measurement was already performed when this thesis was started. The goal of this work is to develop new readout electronics for the scintillation detector, which are compatible with the Phase-2 readout electronics of the \ac{DT} chamber. The most important requirement is the synchronization of the \ac{DT} chamber and the developed scintillator readout system. To achieve this, the new electronics feature the \ac{lpGBT} chip, a high-performance serializer-deserializer device developed at \acs{CERN}. The \ac{lpGBT} allows injection of the hit data into the \ac{DT} backend system, and the reception of a common clock to ensure synchronous operation.
\par After a brief introduction to particle physics (sec.\ \ref{sec:Theory}) and particle detector fundamentals (sec.\ \ref{sec:Detectors}), the two used detectors and their readout electronics are described in detail (secs.\ \ref{sec:DT} and \ref{sec:Scint}). Emphasis is put on the newly developed scintillator readout electronics (sec.\ \ref{sec:Scint-ReadoutBoard}) and corresponding firmware (sec.\ \ref{sec:Scint-ReadoutFirmware}). The reconstruction strategy to obtain \ac{DT} chamber tracks (sec.\ \ref{sec:DTData}) and scintillator strip hits (sec.\ \ref{sec:ScintData}) from the raw recorded data is discussed. To perform the track reconstruction, an algorithm for triggerless analysis of the \ac{DT} data was implemented into a custom analysis framework \cite{dt-scint-analysis-tools-Main}. The analysis strategy itself is based on the \ac{CMS} \ac{DT} local reconstruction \cite{DT-LocalReco}. Finally, the reconstructed tracks and hits of the two detectors are combined in sec.\ \ref{sec:Combined}, and it is evaluated whether the desired synchronization of the two detectors can be achieved (sec.\ \ref{sec:Combined-TemporalMatching}). Also, an independent cross-check of the implemented \ac{DT} reconstruction algorithm is provided by analyzing the geometry of \ac{DT} tracks occurring simultaneously with scintillator hits (sec.\ \ref{sec:Combined-MatchedHitAnalysis}).
