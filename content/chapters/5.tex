\FloatBarrier
\label{sec:Scint}
This chapter focuses on the second muon detector used in this thesis: A scintillation strip detector with \ac{SiPM} photon readout. The readout electronics and firmware was newly developed for this work, with the intention to connect it to the Phase-2 \ac{DT} backend (sec.\ \ref{sec:CMS-Phase2Readout}). The goal is to read out the scintillator and the \ac{DT} chamber together with this system and to demonstrate that a synchronization was actually achieved (sec.\ \ref{sec:Combined}).

\FloatBarrier
\section{Detector setup}
\label{sec:Scint-Setup}
%Mention self-development of detector at institute \cite{BSc-Ehlert} \cite{MSc-Ehlert}.
The detector itself was developed at Physics Institute III A in Aachen over the course of past Bachelor's and Master's theses \cite{BSc-Ehlert,MSc-Ehlert}. The development of the detector was already completed as this thesis begun, therefore the final product could already be used here.
\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Detector layout}
The detector consists of two layers of 16 scintillator strips stacked with perpendicular orientation to each other, each measuring \SI{50}{\centi\meter} in length, \SI{3}{\centi\meter} in width and \SI{0.5}{\centi\meter} in thickness. The assembly gives a sensitive detector area of around $\SI{50}{\centi\meter} \times \SI{50}{\centi\meter}$ divided into $16 \times 16 = 256$ quadratic sectors with a side length of $\SI{3}{\centi\meter}$. The detector material itself is an organic plastic scintillator (sec.\ \ref{sec:Scintillators-Scintillators}) referred to as EJ-200 \cite{Scint-PlasticScint}. The strips are wrapped in Tyvek\textsuperscript{\textregistered} 1442R reflective fabric \cite{Scint-Tyvek}. Light guiding and wavelength shifting fibers are glues into both long edges of each scintillator strip. The casing of the detector does not only provide light isolation from external photons, but also includes walls in between each of the strips, to suppress optical crosstalk between layers \cite{BSc-Ehlert,MSc-Ehlert}. Fig.\ \ref{fig:Scint-Setup-Renderings} shows two renderings of the detector.

\paragraph{Photon detection}
The scintillation photons created in the scintillator are read out by 64 \acp{SiPM} (sec.\ \ref{sec:Scintillators-SiPMs}). One \ac{SiPM} is mounted on both ends of each strip, reading out the photons from the two wavelength-shifting fibers. The model of \ac{SiPM} used is the onsemi\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} J-Series 30035 \cite{Scint-OnsemiSiPM}. The optical coupling between the scintillator and the \ac{SiPM} is accomplished with ELASTOSIL\textsuperscript{\textregistered} RT 604 optical silicon rubber pads \cite{Scint-Elastosil}.

\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Signal preamplifier} A small frontend \ac{PCB} is mounted directly at the scintillator. The \ac{SiPM} itself is mounted on this \ac{PCB}. Additionally, it features a \SI{22}{\decibel} preamplifier, which amplifies the  \ac{SiPM} output signal to measurable voltage ranges. The setup achieves an output amplitude for one \ac{p.e.} (sec.\ \ref{sec:Scintillators-SiPMs}) of $\approx\SI{5}{\milli\volt}$ for an applied bias voltage of \SI{29}{\volt} \cite{MSc-Ehlert}. In addition, the \ac{PCB} hosts a \ac{LED} which may be used to test the photon readout. In this work this feature is however not used. Fig.\ \ref{fig:Scint-Setup-FrontendPCB} shows a picture of one frontend \ac{PCB}.
\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Frontend infrastructure}
Around the sides of the detector, a total of eight infrastructure \acp{PCB} (also called "backbones") are mounted. Each infrastructure board directly connects to eight frontend \acp{PCB}, i.e.\ eight \acp{SiPM}. It supplies the bias voltage as well as the operation voltage for the preamplifier. The amplified signals are provided on the infrastructure \ac{PCB} on \ac{MCX} connectors, so it can be further transmitted to the readout system via coaxial cables. A Raspberry Pi \cite{Raspberry-Pi} is used to control the bias voltage levels of all channels \cite{MSc-Ehlert}.

\paragraph{Operating conditions}
Due to fabrication tolerances, the internal breakdown voltage of the individual \acp{SiPM} varies as much as \SI{0.5}{\volt} for the used model \cite{Scint-OnsemiSiPM}. As the \ac{SiPM} output amplitude varies with the applied bias voltage, one can in principle correct for these fluctuations between \acp{SiPM} by conducting extensive calibration measurements \cite{MSc-Ehlert}. However, measurements in this work do not aim at characterization of the detector performance or precision determination of the cosmic muon rate. Therefore, these measurements were omitted and a constant bias voltage of \SI{29}{\volt} is used.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Overview}
\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{General requirements}
The following requirements were defined for the readout board:
\newline It should feature 32 input channels for analog \ac{SiPM} signals. These signals should be discriminated with a given threshold voltage (cf.\ sec.\ \ref{sec:ReadoutElectronics-General}). A precise timestamp should be assigned to each hit, with a precision of $\mathcal{O}(\SI{1}{\nano\second})$. For calibration and testing purposes, there should also be the capability to send simultaneous testpulses to all input channels. The board should operate in synchronization with the central clock, assign global timestamps to all recorded hits and transfer them to the \ac{DT} backend. The compatibility to the \ac{DT} backend is essential, as the goal of this work is to read out the scintillator together with the \ac{DT} chamber.
\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Readout board}
A new \ac{PCB} was developed to host the readout system according to the requirements listed above. Fig.\ \ref{fig:Scint-Readout-CarrierBoard} shows a picture of the board. It features 32 \ac{MCX} connectors for the \ac{SiPM} input signals. 
\newline The hit data is processed with an AMD\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} Xilinx Zynq\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} UltraScale+\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} MPSoC module \cite{UltraScale-FPGA}. A \ac{SoC} is a module that feature both \ac{PL}, i.e.\ an \ac{FPGA} (sec.\ \ref{sec:ReadoutElectronics-FPGAs}), and a \ac{PS}, i.e.\ one or several microprocessor cores. These modules allow to combine the advantages of \acp{FPGA} with conventional computing and offer fast data interfaces between both units. The concrete device used is the Trentz Elektronik TE-0820 which features an AMD\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} Xilinx Zynq\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} XCZU3EG-1SFVC784E \cite{FPGA-Trentz}.
\newline To forward the acquired data to the backend and to receive the central clock, another \ac{PCB} was developed, the \ac{lpGBT} mezzanine (sec.\ \ref{sec:Scint-lpGBTMezzanine}). The readout board hosts a \ac{FMC} connector to plug the mezzanine onto it.
