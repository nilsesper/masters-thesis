\FloatBarrier
\label{sec:Simulation}

The custom analysis framework developed for this thesis \cite{dt-scint-analysis-tools-Main} allows the \acf{MC} generation of atmospheric muon tracks and the propagation of those tracks through the geometry of both the \ac{DT} chamber and the scintillator. In this way, the expected detector output from atmospheric muons can be estimated.
\par Note that the performed simulation is only considering the detector geometry, and not the physics of the interactions of the atmospheric muons with the surrounding material during their propagation. Also, the physics inside the detectors themselves is not simulated. Instead, a simple geometric evaluation of the muon track intersecting the sensitive detector area is performed, and the detector hit timestamp is calculated from the intersection point. The simulation does not model any noise in the detector or the readout systems, which is present in the real setup.
\par Therefore, it is clear that a bias of the simulation is expected. Since the simulation results should only be used as a rough basis for comparison with the data, the bias is accepted, but has to be kept in mind when comparing the results to real data.

\FloatBarrier
\section{Detector geometry}
\label{sec:Simulation-Geometry}

The detector geometry of the combined setup, consisting of the \ac{DT} chamber and the scintillator, is realized in the simulation. The base points of the two detectors are chosen similarly to what was discussed in sec.\ \ref{sec:Combined-Setup}, with the scintillator being positioned around \SI{1}{\meter} below the \ac{DT} chamber.

\section{Muon track generation}
\label{sec:Simulation-Generation}

%% total simulated tracks: 4198026
%% simulated duration: 1000.2017319957001 s
The implemented muon generator works by simply sampling the track parameters from the expected distributions of atmospheric muons (sec.\ \ref{sec:AtmosphericMuons-Dependencies}) with a \ac{MC} method. A simulated muon track has the same parameter as a reconstructed \ac{DT} global track (sec.\ \ref{sec:DTData-GlobalTrackBuilding}): The arrival time $T_0$, the azimuth and zenith angles $(\phi,\theta)$, and a base point $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$. Note that this simulation assumes that the distributions of all track parameters are independent of each other.
\paragraph{Generator base area}
The generated muon tracks have base points that lie in a fixed rectangular area. The $z$-coordinate of this area is fixed, and the $(x,y)$ coordinates of the base point are sampled from a uniform distribution over a given range. In the case presented here, the area is chosen as the sensitive area of the \ac{DT} chamber, plus a margin of \SI{1.5}{\meter} in all directions. The additional margin ensures that also angled tracks are hitting the edges of the \ac{DT} chamber and therefore the detector is uniformly illuminated in a good approximation. The $z$ height is chosen as the lowest point of the \ac{DT} chamber in the selected coordinate frame.
\par Since the scintillator is a smaller detector, which is placed in the acceptance range of the \ac{DT} chamber, it is also illuminated with this choice of the base area.
\paragraph{Angular distribution}
In the theory chapter, it was discussed that the azimuth angle $\phi$ is approximately uniformly distributed (sec.\ \ref{sec:AtmosphericMuons-Dependencies}). Therefore, this variable is sampled from a uniform distribution in the range $[0, 2\pi)$.
\par The zenith angle $\theta$ for atmospheric muons in a good approximation follows a $\propto \cos^2\theta$ distribution (sec.\ \ref{sec:AtmosphericMuons-Dependencies}). The muon generator samples zenith angles from this distribution in the range $[0, \pi/2)$, i.e.\ over the full half sphere.
\paragraph{Arrival time}
The vertical flux of atmospheric muons is given in the theory chapter as $\Phi(\theta=\SI{0}{\radian}) \approx \SI{70}{\meter^{-2} \second^{-1} \steradian^{-1}}$ (eq.\ \ref{eq:CosmicMuon-VerticalFlux} in sec.\ \ref{sec:AtmosphericMuons-Dependencies}). By integration of this flux over the polar angle of the full half-sphere, one obtains the expected muon rate per area $\dot{N}/A$, cumulated for all angular directions. Note that one has to respect the angular dependence of the muon flux $\Phi(\theta)=\Phi(\theta=\SI{0}{\radian})\cdot\cos^2\theta$ (eq.\ \ref{eq:CosmicMuon-ZenithDependence}):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dot{N}}{A} = \int d\Omega\; \Phi(\theta) = \int_{0}^{2\pi} d\phi \int_{0}^{\pi/2} d\theta\; \sin\theta\cdot\cos^2\theta\cdot\Phi(\theta=\SI{0}{\radian}) \approx \SI{147}{\second^{-1}\meter^{-2}}.
\end{equation}
The obtained value is now the muon rate per area to be used in the simulation, cumulated over all angles.
\par From the base area $A_\text{base} = \Delta x_\text{base}\cdot\Delta y_\text{base}$ in which the muons are generated, one can calculate the mean muon rate $\left\langle \dot{N} \right\rangle$ expected from the setup:
\begin{equation}
\left\langle \dot{N} \right\rangle = \frac{\dot{N}}{A} \cdot A_\text{base}.
\end{equation}
Now, the timestamps of the muon tracks are generated. It is known that for a Poisson distribution, the time differences of events are exponentially distributed \cite{Book-KolanoskiWermes}. Therefore, the timestamp differences between the muon hits can be generated by sampling from this exponential distribution (which contains the rate $\left\langle \dot{N} \right\rangle$). By summing the time differences between all previous events, one can then obtain the timestamps for all generated muon tracks.
\paragraph{Simulated dataset}
For the simulation presented in this thesis, the simulation time interval was set to $T_\text{sim}\approx\SI{1000}{\second}=\SI{16}{\min}\;\SI{40}{\second}$. In the performed run, the generated number of muon tracks is $N=\num{4198026}$. This track count is considered sufficient to give results with acceptably low statistical uncertainties.

\section{Propagation through DT chamber}
\label{sec:Simulation-DT}

The generated muon tracks are propagated through the \ac{DT} chamber geometry to calculate the drift cell hits. As already explained, the simulation does not model the physics of the muon interactions with the surrounding material, e.g.\ energy loss or scattering processes. Instead, it simply propagates the straight track through the specified geometry. Note that the simulation treats the muon as infinitely fast, i.e.\ it neglects the time of flight of the particle.
\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Drift cell hit evaluation}
Generally, it is clear that when a muon track passes through a drift cell, it can create ionization over the full length of the track. However, in accordance with the assumptions of the \ac{DT} superlayer track fit (sec.\ \ref{sec:DTData-SLPatternFitting-Strategy}), this is not modeled. Instead, only the position of the track at the $z$ height of the wire, i.e.\ at the center height of each layer, is considered. This means that each muon track can generate only one hit in each layer, if it intersects the \ac{DT} chamber.
\par When comparing the simulation results with the measurement, this has to be taken into account. For the real data, the ionization, of course, happens over the full track length, which means that a deviation is expected between data and simulation. This is especially the case for inclined tracks, as for these tracks, the closest distance between the track and the anode wire might no longer be on the horizontal axis.
\par It is clear that in reality, the drift cell can of course also be triggered by ionization in the gas volume, which is not at the height of the wire. This is not considered in this approximation.
\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Modeling of dead cells and cell efficiency}
The dead cells observed with the real \ac{DT} chamber (tab.\ \ref{tab:DTData-DeadWires}) should be considered in the simulation as well. This is realized by simply removing all hits in the respective dead cells from the obtained set of simulated muon track hits.
\par Also, the non-ideal cell efficiency is taken into account in the simulation. This is implemented by \enquote{dicing} a random value in the range $[0,1]$ each time a cell is hit. Only if the diced value is smaller than the cell efficiency $\varepsilon_\text{cell}$ (sec.\ \ref{sec:CMS-DTPerformance}), the cell hit is accepted.

\section{Propagation through scintillator}
\label{sec:Simulation-Scint}

Similar to the \ac{DT} chamber, the simulated muon tracks are also propagated through the scintillator, which is placed in the detector geometry (sec.\ \ref{sec:Simulation-Geometry}). The time of flight of the muons is neglected, as well as the physical interactions of the muon with the surrounding material, similar to sec.\ \ref{sec:Simulation-DT}.
\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Scintillator strip hit evaluation}
For both layers of the scintillator, the intersection point of the muon track at the central $z$ height is used to evaluate which strip has been hit. This is similar to the procedure with the \ac{DT} chamber, where the track intersection is evaluated at the height of the anode wire in the cell (sec.\ \ref{sec:Simulation-DT}).
