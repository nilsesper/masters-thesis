\section{Timing calibration with testpulses}

Fig.\ \ref{fig:DTData-Testpulse-LatencyDifference} shows an oscilloscope screenshot of the measurement of the testpulse output latency for both \ac{OBDT} flavors. It is evident that the testpulses are not issued at the same time, although both \acp{OBDT} were programmed to do so. As explained in sec.\ \ref{sec:DTData-Testpulse-Generation}, this is due to the differences in analog testpulse circuitry. From the cursor in the screenshot, the time difference is estimated as $T_\text{testpulse, theta} - T_\text{testpulse, phi} = \SI{116}{\nano\second}$. One conservatively assumes an uncertainty of $\SI{1}{\nano\second}$, given the step size of the cursor of $\SI{2}{\nano\second}$.
\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{DTData-TestpulseLatencyDifference.PNG}
    \caption{Measurement of the latency between the \ac{OBDT} phi (yellow) and theta (magenta) testpulse output with an oscilloscope. The time difference can be read off from the cursor $\Delta t$ value in the bottom left.}
    \label{fig:DTData-Testpulse-LatencyDifference}
\end{figure}

Fig.\ \ref{fig:DTData-CellHitDifference-ExpFit} shows the result of an exponential fit to the tail of the hit time difference distribution for $\Delta T_\text{cell} \geq \SI{1000}{\nano\second}$. From the residual plot and the goodness-of-fit variable (which lies in the order of one) one can conclude that the exponential behavior of the time difference $\Delta T_\text{cell}$ is indeed a valid assumption. The obtained exponential fit is used as "background" estimation for the Poisson noise events, as discussed in sec.\ \ref{sec:DTData-CellHits-SecondaryHits}
